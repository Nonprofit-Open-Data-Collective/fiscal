Purpose

dimension-discovery-2023.Rmd found four stable dimensions in the 2023 full-990 filers. Three have a clear healthy direction; one describes the business model. This document freezes that structure into a reusable measurement model:

Dimension Indicators Healthy direction
Leverage debt/assets, payables share of short-term capital lower
Performance & growth surplus margin, return on assets, net-asset, revenue, and asset growth higher
Liquidity & reserves months of cash, liquid months, assets/revenue, current ratio higher
Asset structure (context) cash share vs. land & buildings share none

The composite fiscal health index averages the three directional dimensions. Asset structure and the standalone ratios (self-sufficiency, admin share, fundraising share) are scored and reported, but they get no weight.

Key ideas

  • A fitted model is a set of stored parameters. It records percentile bounds, transformation constants, centers and scales, indicator directions, and dimension coefficients. Scoring new data reuses them; nothing is re-estimated.
  • Orientation: before averaging, every indicator in a dimension is flipped so that higher means healthier. For the context dimension, higher simply means a more liquid asset base.
  • Standardized-mean scoring: a dimension score is the average of its oriented z-scores, rescaled to mean 0 and SD 1. It is transparent, and here it is nearly identical to PCA or factor-weighted scores.
  • Context vs. health: a stable dimension without a healthy direction is scored and reported, but it has zero weight in the composite.
  • Holdout check: fit the model on half the organizations and score the other half. The scores should match the full-sample fit.
  • Peer-group percentile: an organization’s rank among similar organizations (same subsector and size band) rather than among all filers. A hospital at its peers’ median is typical for a hospital, even if hospitals as a group score low nationally.

Decision 1: Set every direction explicitly.

Several indicators are not in the package registry (payables_una, the growth rates, the bounded and symmetric reformulations). Two differ from it on purpose:

  • assets_rev is higher, not lower. The registry reads assets per dollar of revenue as asset inefficiency. Empirically it loads with months of cash and liquid reserves: organizations with more assets per dollar of activity have more resources relative to operations. Oriented lower, it would cancel the other reserve indicators.
  • The asset-structure dimension is oriented, even though it has no healthy direction. Cash share and land share load with opposite signs. Leaving both unoriented would make them cancel. Orienting cash higher and land lower defines the score as “more liquid, less fixed asset base”. That describes the business model and says nothing about health.

The three standalone ratios stay context, i.e. unoriented. The fit records a warning for them, and that is intended.

Decision 2: Score dimensions as standardized means.

Why: The standardized mean correlates at least 0.97 with factor-weighted scores and 1.00 with PCA-weighted scores.

Factor weights would concentrate performance & growth on return on assets and net-asset growth, and reserves on months of cash. That makes the scores depend on a single year’s estimates. Equal weights are transparent and don’t change between reference years.

Equal weighting does give balance-sheet growth (net-asset and asset growth) two of the five performance indicators. The comparison above shows that choice changes scores very little.

Decision 3: Weight the composite 1/3 each on leverage, performance & growth, and reserves; give asset structure zero weight.

Why: No outcome in a single cross-section can calibrate weights, so equal weights are the defensible default. Zero weight for asset structure follows from its lack of a healthy direction. Scoring it anyway keeps it available as a stratifier or control.

Note on the sign of leverage: fh_leverage is oriented, so higher means less leveraged. All three weighted scores read “higher is healthier”.

To score another tax year:

  1. Build its ratio frame with build_ratio_frame().
  2. Apply norm_spec with apply_winsor_normalize().
  3. Score with fit, then index with weights.

One caveat. Conditional imputation is re-estimated within each year’s frame. Only the normalization and model parameters are frozen.

8. Peer groups: subsector and size

National scores answer “how does this organization compare with all full-990 filers?” Section 5.4 showed that scores differ systematically by size and business model. A hospital, a food bank, and a business league run different balance sheets by design. Peer-group percentiles answer “how does this organization compare with organizations like it?”

# Percentile of each organization within its peer group's *reference set*. For
# a sector x size cell, the reference set is the cell. For a fallback group
# (small cell), it is every organization in the whole sector or subsector, not
# just the members of the small cell. Ties get the average rank.
reference_percentile <- function(x, group, reference_rows) {
  out <- numeric(length(x))
  for (g in unique(group)) {
    target <- which(group == g)
    v <- sort(x[reference_rows(g)])
    below    <- findInterval(x[target], v, left.open = TRUE)   # count < x
    at_below <- findInterval(x[target], v)                     # count <= x
    out[target] <- pmax(1, ceiling(100 * ((below + at_below + 1) / 2) / length(v)))
  }
  out
}
peer_reference <- function(g) {
  if (g == "All full-990 filers") return(seq_along(peer_group))
  which(cell == g | peer_sector == g | peer_sector_pooled == g)
}

Decision 5: Peer group = sector × size band, with at least 250 organizations.

Size is part of the peer definition because Section 5.4 showed scores fall steadily with size, through leverage and reserves. Three bands match the size groups used throughout: under $500k, $500k–$5M, and $5M+ in total expenses. Cells below 250 fall back to the whole sector. What the splits do:

  • Overall shift. Across the 147,229 organizations in split subsectors, 10.6% move 10 or more percentile points and 2.6% move 20 or more.
  • Where the split matters most. Major group matters most within human services, explaining 5% of composite variance there, and least within environment & animals (0%). How to read peer percentiles:

  • Peer percentile 50 means typical for that peer group. The last table shows how different “typical” is across groups in national terms.
  • Use national scores to measure sector-wide conditions or to compare groups. Use peer percentiles to judge one organization, set benchmarks, or flag outliers within a field.
  • Use both for screening. A low peer percentile flags organizations that are weak even by their sector’s norms. A low national percentile with a typical peer percentile says the weakness comes from the sector’s financial model. Report both.

8.5 Storing peer norms for new data

Percentiles for a later year, or for a single organization, should be read off the 2023 reference distributions rather than re-ranked within the new data. We store the 1st–99th percentile cut points of each score in each peer group.

peer_norms <- do.call(rbind, lapply(unique(peer_group), function(g) {
  rows <- peer_reference(g)   # full reference set, including for fallback groups
  do.call(rbind, lapply(peer_cols, function(v) data.frame(
    peer_group = g, score = v, n = length(rows), percentile = 1:99,
    cut = quantile(indexed[[v]][rows], (1:99) / 100, names = FALSE))))
}))

lookup_peer_percentile <- function(score_value, score_name, group, norms) {
  vapply(seq_along(score_value), function(i) {
    cuts <- norms$cut[norms$peer_group == group[i] & norms$score == score_name]
    as.numeric(findInterval(score_value[i], cuts) + 1)
  }, numeric(1))
}

10. Limitations and next steps

  • Validity beyond face validity. The quintile checks use conditions that partly feed the index. The decisive test is predictive: do low 2023 scores precede deficits, declining net assets, or stopping filing in 2024–2025? That needs later tax years.
  • Weights. Equal weights are a default, not an estimate. Once outcomes are available, weights can be calibrated (for example, to predict insolvency) and passed to fiscal_weights().
  • Finer peers. Every subsector that spans several NTEE major groups is now split. The remaining large peer sectors can’t be split at the major-group level:
    • Education (one major group, B) could be split by NTEE division, e.g. elementary & secondary schools (B20s) vs. student and educational services (B80s–B90s).
    • Unclassified 501(c)(3)s have no NTEE code at all. The IRS public-charity (foundation) code would separate schools, churches, hospitals, and supporting organizations hidden in that group.
    • Arts and religion are single major groups, and more homogeneous.
  • NTEE coverage. Close to a third of full-990 filers are unclassified in the BMF. Classifying them from mission text or program descriptions would move unclassified 501(c)(3)s into real subsectors.
  • Package port. build-ratio-frame.R (rules, imputation, growth) and fit_winsor_normalize() / apply_winsor_normalize() belong in the package, so the model can be refit and applied without the dev scripts.
