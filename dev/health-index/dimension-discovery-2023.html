Purpose

fiscal-health-index-2023.Rmd found two dimensions: Reserves & Solvency and Operating Performance. That result depended on screening out most of the candidate ratios. The AA/NH/PI study (Urban Institute) found four: growth potential, leverage, solvency, and organizational slack. vignette("variance-preserving-normalization") showed that the difference comes mostly from which ratios are measured, and how, not from the transformation. It then built a measurement model that keeps every candidate ratio defined for every organization:

  • bounded or symmetric reformulations where the direction of an undefined case is known (negative net assets, a non-positive growth base);
  • stochastic conditional imputation where it is not (no current liabilities, unreported functional expenses);
  • 1st/99th percentile winsorizing, then asinh for ratios that stay skewed, then a z-score.

This document reruns dimension discovery on that frame for all 2023 full-990 filers, and records how each decision was made.

Key ideas

  • Measurement model first: decide how each ratio is defined, filled in, and transformed before factoring. Those choices determine which correlations the model can find.
  • Parallel analysis and MAP: two rules for how many dimensions to keep. Parallel analysis keeps components that beat random data; MAP stops when another component no longer removes shared (partial) correlation.
  • Oblique rotation (oblimin): lets dimensions correlate, which is realistic for financial constructs. The component correlation matrix shows how much they overlap.
  • Tucker congruence: similarity of two loading patterns. At 0.95 or above, the dimension has effectively reproduced.
  • Context dimension: a stable empirical dimension with no healthy direction. Use it to describe or stratify organizations, not in a health score.

The automatic triage flags four things:

  • debt_una is redundant with debt_assets (r = 0.95). Once negative unrestricted net assets are placed at the worst end, leverage on unrestricted equity and debt-to-assets rank organizations almost identically. Keeping both would double-weight one idea.
  • self_suff, admin_exp, and fundr_exp share almost no variance with anything else (communality ≤ 0.05).
  • land_assets is marked standalone because of its low communality in the preliminary screen.
  • Asset-composition ratios (cash_assets, land_assets, assets_rev) have individual KMO just below 0.50.

The preliminary communality screen fits only 3 factors. Section 3 shows that a fourth component is defined by land_assets and cash_assets. So a three-factor communality is not a fair test of whether land_assets shares variance, and we override it.

Decision D: Retain four dimensions.

Why:

  • Criteria disagree, as they usually do. PCA parallel analysis and the Kaiser rule keep 4 and 4; factor-analytic parallel analysis keeps 5. Velicer’s MAP reaches its minimum with 1 component(s). MAP is known to under-extract when each dimension has only two to four indicators, and here it mainly signals that a general factor sits behind the ratios. That is consistent with building a composite, but it is not a reason to collapse distinct constructs.
  • Three components merge two clusters. Leverage and liquidity collapse into one bipolar component, even though the four-component solution shows they separate cleanly.
  • Five components trade one coherent dimension for a finer split. The liquidity block splits into short-term liquidity (current ratio, months of cash) and accumulated reserves (liquid months, asset intensity). Asset structure breaks up: land share becomes a near-singleton, and cash share cross-loads on both liquidity components. That split is substantively plausible and worth testing on panel data. At four components, though, every dimension is an interpretable multi-indicator cluster.
  • Simple structure. In the four-component solution, every cluster has at least two indicators with primary loadings above 0.75. Only current and assets_rev cross-load.
  • EFA agrees. Common-factor analysis, which ignores unique variance, recovers the same grouping. The current ratio and liquid months take small secondary loadings on leverage.

If the automated fiscal_dimensions() count differs from four, the fit below uses four explicitly. The comparison above is the basis for that choice.

With every candidate included, PCA parallel analysis adds a component for fundraising share, self-sufficiency, and admin share. That is expected: PCA gives variance to any variable not explained by the others. But the component is not a shared construct. Communalities for these ratios are near zero, and the component breaks into singletons as soon as a sixth is allowed. In 2023, organizational slack does not appear as a dimension once program share (the arithmetic complement of admin and fundraising shares) is removed.

4. Stability

A dimension worth naming should reappear:

  • in independent halves of the data;
  • in organizations of very different size;
  • under alternative transformations;
  • in a different tax year.

We compare loading matrices with Tucker’s congruence coefficient, matching components across solutions by the best permutation. Values ≥ 0.95 mean the factors are effectively equal; 0.85–0.94 means fair similarity.

perms <- function(n) {
  if (n == 1) return(list(1L))
  out <- list()
  for (i in seq_len(n)) for (p in perms(n - 1)) out[[length(out) + 1]] <- c(i, setdiff(seq_len(n), i)[p])
  out
}
loadings_k4 <- function(df) unclass(principal(df[retained], nfactors = 4, rotate = "oblimin")$loadings)
label_components <- function(L) {
  anchors <- c(leverage = "debt_assets", performance = "return_assets",
               reserves = "months_cash", asset_structure = "land_assets")
  lab <- vapply(seq_len(ncol(L)), function(j) {
    a <- names(anchors)[which.max(abs(L[anchors, j]))]
    a
  }, "")
  colnames(L) <- lab
  L
}
congruence <- function(A, B) {
  C <- abs(psych::factor.congruence(A, B))
  P <- perms(ncol(B))
  best <- P[[which.max(vapply(P, function(p) sum(C[cbind(seq_len(nrow(C)), p)]), 0))]]
  out <- C[cbind(seq_len(nrow(C)), best)]
  names(out) <- colnames(label_components(A))
  out
}

Each label names what the indicators have in common: their accounting source, what they respond to, and the question they answer. “Higher is healthier” is stated only where the direction is unambiguous.

Leverage (debt_assets, payables_una)

  • Source. Balance-sheet claims. Debt-to-assets is the share of all assets financed by liabilities. The payables share is the share of short-term capital (payables plus unrestricted equity) that is owed to vendors and grantees.
  • Why separate from reserves. The three-component solution merges them. In four, leverage stands on its own, correlates only modestly (negatively) with reserves, and reproduces across sizes and years. Organizations can hold large reserves and significant debt (property financed by mortgages), or neither.
  • Healthy direction. Lower. High scores mean creditors, not the organization, have the claim on its resources.
  • Relation to the AA/NH/PI study. This corresponds to leverage. Unlike the study, debt-to-assets belongs here and not in a separate solvency dimension. Once negative net assets are handled, debt-to-equity-style ratios and debt-to-assets measure one construct.

Operating performance (surplus_margin, return_assets, na_growth)

  • Source. Income-statement flows and their result: this year’s surplus relative to revenue and assets, and the resulting change in net assets.
  • Healthy direction. Higher, within reason. A single deficit year is not distress.
  • Relation to the AA/NH/PI study. Growth potential. That dimension also included asset and revenue growth. Section 6 adds those and finds that they join this dimension.

Liquidity and reserves (months_cash, liquid_months, assets_rev, current)

  • Source. Stocks of liquid and unrestricted resources measured against spending: months of cash and receivables, months of liquid unrestricted net assets, assets per dollar of revenue, and current assets per dollar of current liabilities.
  • Why assets_rev loads here. It measures the stock of resources relative to the flow of activity. Organizations with more assets per dollar of revenue carry larger reserves relative to operations. It also cross-loads on asset structure, because what those assets are (cash or buildings) matters too.
  • Why current is the weakest member. Its denominator is a liability, so it loads almost equally (with opposite sign) on leverage. It is best treated as a secondary indicator of this dimension.
  • Healthy direction. Higher, up to a point. The index doesn’t penalize hoarding.
  • Relation to the AA/NH/PI study. The resilience half of solvency. The study’s solvency dimension mixed reserves, debt-to-assets, and asset mix.

Asset structure (cash_assets vs. land_assets)

  • Source. Composition of the asset base: liquid (cash and savings) versus fixed (land, buildings, equipment).
  • Caveat. Cash and property are shares of the same total, so part of their negative correlation is compositional. That is why the individual KMO values sit just below 0.50.
  • Healthy direction. None. A capital-intensive organization (hospital, school, housing provider) isn’t less healthy than a cash-based one; it has a different business model.
  • Recommendation. Treat asset structure as a context dimension: a stratifier or control. Don’t add it to a directional health composite.
  • Relation to the AA/NH/PI study. The asset-mix part of solvency and of organizational slack (land-to-assets and asset-to-revenue loaded on both).

Standalone measures (not dimensions)

  • Administrative share, fundraising share, self-sufficiency. They share almost no variance with financial condition or with each other. Report them as descriptors of expense allocation and revenue model.
  • Organizational slack. The study’s slack dimension paired the administrative-expense ratio with the program-efficiency ratio. Those two are complements by construction, so the pair forms a component regardless of how organizations behave. Without the complement, no slack dimension appears.

6. Adding revenue and asset growth

The AA/NH/PI study’s growth potential dimension combined one-year asset growth and one-year revenue growth with net profit and operating margins. This section tests whether growth measures join the performance dimension here too.

Measurement model for growth. A panel isn’t strictly needed. One 2023 return already reports both years:

  • Revenue: Part I lists prior-year and current-year total revenue (F9_01_REV_TOT_PY, F9_01_REV_TOT_CY).
  • Assets: Part X lists beginning- and end-of-year total assets (F9_10_ASSET_TOT_BOY, F9_10_ASSET_TOT_EOY).

Both use the same symmetric growth rate as net-asset growth: change ÷ mean(abs(new), abs(old)). It is bounded in [−2, 2], defined whenever either year is nonzero, and 0 when both are zero.

A blank prior-year revenue means not reported (for example, first-year filers), not zero. It is filled from the organization’s 2022 return where one exists; the rest are imputed conditionally, like every other undefined ratio.

What the growth test shows.

  • Both growth measures join performance. In the four-component solution, asset growth loads 0.87 and revenue growth 0.59 on the performance component. Neither loads above 0.14 anywhere else, and EFA assigns them to the same factor.
  • The other three dimensions are unchanged. Leverage, liquidity and reserves, and asset structure keep the same indicators and near-identical loadings. Performance loadings for the original ratios barely move.
  • This matches the AA/NH/PI study’s growth potential. In that study, asset growth loaded highest and revenue growth lowest, with the margins in between. The same ordering appears here.
  • Revenue growth is the weakest member. Its communality is 0.21. Revenue fluctuates with one-time grants and gifts, while asset and net-asset growth accumulate the whole year’s result. When a fifth component is allowed, revenue growth pairs with surplus margin (the two revenue-statement measures) and separates from the balance-sheet growth measures. That split is a possible refinement, not a replacement.
  • Asset growth and net-asset growth overlap strongly (r = 0.77), because assets = liabilities + net assets. The overlap is below the redundancy cutoff, and asset growth also reflects growth financed by borrowing. Keep both, but note that balance-sheet growth carries two of the five performance indicators.

Decision F: Add revenue growth and asset growth to the performance dimension, and relabel it Performance & growth.

The structure is stable across random halves, organization size, and transformation. It is also identical whether growth comes from the single return or from the 2022–2023 panel.

8. Next steps

  • Fit the measurement model with fiscal_health_fit(). Use the three directional dimensions:

    Dimension Direction
    leverage lower is better
    performance & growth (including rev_growth, asset_growth) higher
    reserves higher

    Keep asset_structure and the three standalone measures as reported, non-weighted columns.
